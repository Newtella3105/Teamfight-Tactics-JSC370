Trait Win Rate and Model Summary

1 Champion and Trait Win Rate Analysis

So far, we’ve explored how champion stats like health, damage, and DPS scale across costs and star levels in Sets 12 and 13. However, high stats don’t necessarily translate to better performance. To assess how effective champions and traits actually are in-game, we now incorporate win rate as our performance metric.

Win rate in this analysis refers to the Top 1 Rate, derived from thousands of real team compositions. Each champion’s win rate is averaged across all compositions in which it appears. This allows us to examine whether certain champions or traits consistently contribute to victory — regardless of raw stats.

2 Summary

This project investigated how champion stats, traits, and game design changes between Set 12 and Set 13 in Teamfight Tactics (TFT) influence team performance. By analyzing thousands of real ranked team compositions and applying multiple modeling approaches, we identified key structural changes and predictors of success in the evolving TFT metagame.

2.1 Key Findings

  • Champion Stat Scaling
    Champion stats (Health, Damage, DPS) scale positively with both cost and star level in both sets. However, Set 13 introduces a sharper stat gradient, primarily driven by the addition of 6-cost champions, which significantly outpace lower-cost units in raw stats. This supports the hypothesis that Set 13 meaningfully expanded the power curve.

  • Normalized Stat Efficiency
    After adjusting for the gold required to upgrade units (i.e., total cost at each star level), 1-cost champions provide the best stat-per-gold efficiency. Normalized stats decline as unit cost increases, suggesting that higher-cost champions are less efficient, despite being more powerful in absolute terms.

  • Stat Progression Consistency
    Stat progression across star levels is remarkably consistent across sets, indicating that design shifts in Set 13 built upon existing scaling rules rather than redefining them. The key difference lies in the expanded ceiling rather than altered stat progression logic.

  • Trait Dominance over Raw Stats
    Predictive models consistently identified traits as more important than raw stats in explaining win rate and top-four rate. This underscores the central role of trait synergies, which amplify performance through interactions between units, rather than individual power alone.

  • Model Performance and Complexity
    Among all model types tested—Linear Models (LM), Generalized Additive Models (GAM), and Random Forests (RF)—Random Forest models using trait-only features performed best, achieving the highest \(R^2\) and lowest RMSE. This confirms that nonlinear, interaction-based models are better suited for capturing the combinatorial complexity of TFT.

  • Top Traits by Set
    Trait importance varied across sets, reflecting shifting metas and design goals:

    • Set 12: Vanguard, Blaster, and Honeymancy were key contributors to winning teams.
    • Set 13: Artillerist, Ambusher, and Experiment emerged as the most influential traits.

2.2 Overall Conclusion

The introduction of 6-cost champions in Set 13 marked a significant escalation in power, reshaping the competitive landscape. However, this power spike did not break existing game balance mechanisms; rather, it layered new strategic depth onto the existing framework. Across both sets, traits remained the dominant factor in team success, validating the game’s emphasis on synergy over raw strength.

2.3 Broader Implications and Limitations

This analysis highlights the strength of trait-based design in sustaining strategic variety across TFT sets. However, it is limited by its reliance on composition-level data, which prevents precise measurement of individual unit contributions. Moreover, model predictions are constrained by the accuracy and completeness of scraped data from third-party sources. Future analyses could benefit from player-level match data or official Riot APIs to improve granularity and causal inference.
